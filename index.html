Tax calculation details

Taxable income = state pension + the taxable portion of private pension drawdown + savings interest (above the Personal Savings Allowance). Net salary and savings drawdown are excluded.

The pension pot is treated as uncrystallised: the tax-free % below is deducted from each drawdown, and only the remainder is taxable.

Who pays. The private pension and the two "You" state pensions are taxed on person 1 (the older partner, who owns the pot). The spouse's state pension is taxed on person 2. Savings interest is treated as jointly held and split equally. Each person gets their own allowance and bands.

When. Tax is charged on a cumulative UK tax-year basis (April–March), the same way PAYE works: income accrues across the year and each quarter is charged the movement in the running total. One personal allowance therefore covers the year's actual income, and a quarter can show a refund if an earlier quarter over-collected. Because tax years straddle calendar years, the tax on an annual row will not always match that row's own income.

Savings interest stacks on top of pension income and is relieved at 0% by two allowances before any tax is charged: the savings starting-rate band below (reduced £-for-£ by non-savings income above the personal allowance, so it disappears once pension income exceeds allowance + band), then a Personal Savings Allowance of £1,000 (basic rate) / £500 (higher rate) / £0 (additional rate). The personal-allowance taper above £100k is not modelled — this is an estimate only.
